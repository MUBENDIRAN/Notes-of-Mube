A short history, as the notes tell it

  • 2004. The first AWS service in the notes is Amazon SQS, the queue.
  • 2006. Amazon S3 and Amazon EC2.

The rest of the catalogue hangs off that idea: storage and servers first, then the managed services that mean you do not run the queue, the database, or the load balancer yourself.

Cloud computing

The definition in the notes is the one to memorise. Cloud computing is on-demand delivery of IT resources over the internet, with pay-as-you-go pricing. You do not buy a room of servers for a peak that might arrive. You request capacity when you need it, and you stop paying for it when you release it.

Where the workload can run

ModelMeaning to keep
CloudThe workload runs in AWS.
On-premisesThe workload runs in your own data centre.
HybridThe two are connected. Outposts, Direct Connect, VPN, and Storage Gateway are the services these notes use for that pattern.
Multi-cloudMore than one cloud provider. The notes mention it in the same breath as hybrid. They are related, and they are not the same: hybrid is cloud plus your own estate; multi-cloud is more than one cloud.

Why the notes say you would bother

  1. Trade a fixed expense for a variable one.
  2. Benefit from AWS’s economies of scale.
  3. Stop guessing how much capacity you will need.
  4. Move faster. The notes call this speed and agility.
  5. Stop spending money to run and maintain data centres.
  6. Go global in minutes, by using more than one Region.

The notes add a seventh point: Availability Zones and fault tolerance. An Availability Zone is a distinct location inside a Region, with its own power and its own failure domain. You put copies of a workload in more than one zone so one power or network event does not take the application with it. That is the mechanism behind the resilience the six benefits assume, rather than a separate pricing feature.

Remember: six benefits, in the order above, plus Availability Zones as the way a Region survives a failure in one site.

LevelExamplesWhat you still do
UnmanagedEC2Operating system, network, firewall, platform, application.
ManagedElastic Load Balancing, SQS, SNSYou use the service. AWS runs the infrastructure under it.
Fully managedLambdaYou provide code. You do not provision a server.

Regions, zones, and edge locations

A Region is a geographic area. Availability Zones are the isolated sites inside it. An edge location is a site used to serve content closer to users, which is the infrastructure behind CloudFront.

The notes give four reasons to pick a Region:

  1. Compliance. Some data has to stay in a particular place.
  2. Proximity. Closer to the users means lower latency.
  3. Feature availability. Not every service exists in every Region, and the list changes.
  4. Price. The same instance does not cost the same amount in every Region.

Scaling, in words the exam will use

Scalability is room to grow over time. Elasticity is changing that capacity on demand. The notes then swap two labels. Use these:

PhraseMeaning
Scale up, verticalA larger instance. More CPU or memory. An early page of the notes already defines vertical scaling this way.
Scale out, horizontalMore instances. This is what an Auto Scaling group does.
Correction. One page of the source says scaling up is horizontal and scaling out is vertical. That pairing is reversed. The Auto Scaling page repeats the correction next to the minimum, desired, and maximum capacities.

How the service pages are grouped

Compute runs from EC2 through containers, Lambda, and the simpler platforms. Networking is the VPC and every way into it, then DNS and the edge. Storage splits into block, object, and file. Databases cover relational, NoSQL, cache, document, and graph. The AI pages follow the three tiers in the notes: ready-made services, SageMaker, and foundation models. Analytics follows the pipeline from ingest to a dashboard. Security and governance are separated so detection is not mixed up with account structure. Migration ends with the physical devices and the file-transfer options. The last group is the developer tools, the business applications, and the Well-Architected review.
